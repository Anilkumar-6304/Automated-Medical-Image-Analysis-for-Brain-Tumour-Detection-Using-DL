🧠 Brain Tumor Detection Model Information

This system utilizes a deep learning–based Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for the automated detection and classification of brain tumors from MRI images. By analyzing structural and textural patterns within brain scans, the model assists in identifying abnormal growths and categorizing them into clinically relevant classes. The objective of this system is to support radiologists and clinicians in early-stage tumor screening and decision-making.

Dataset

The model is trained on a curated MRI brain image dataset containing multiple tumor categories. Each image is labeled into one of four classes: "No Tumor", "Pituitary Tumor", "Meningioma", or "Glioma". The dataset includes scans with varying contrast, orientations, and resolutions, enabling the model to generalize effectively across diverse patient data.

Preprocessing

Prior to model training and inference, all MRI images are resized to a uniform dimension (150x150 pixels) to maintain consistency. Pixel intensity values are normalized to enhance convergence during training. These preprocessing steps help reduce noise, improve feature extraction, and increase the reliability of tumor classification.

Model Architecture

The core of the system is a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) designed for medical image analysis. It consists of stacked convolutional and pooling layers that learn spatial features such as edges, shapes, and tumor boundaries. The extracted features are flattened and passed through fully connected layers, with a softmax output layer that predicts one of the four brain tumor classes.

Prediction & Interpretation

When an MRI image is submitted, the model computes the probability score for each tumor class. The class with the highest probability is selected as the final prediction. Along with the predicted label, the system provides interpretive suggestions and medical guidance, helping users understand the result and the recommended next steps for clinical consultation.

Applications

  • Assist radiologists in preliminary brain tumor screening.
  • Support early detection and classification of brain tumors.
  • Provide an educational platform for medical imaging research.
  • Enable AI-assisted diagnosis in remote or resource-limited settings.

Limitations & Notes

This system is intended as a decision-support tool and should not replace professional medical diagnosis. Final clinical decisions must be made by qualified healthcare professionals. Model accuracy may vary depending on MRI quality, scan type, and imaging artifacts. Continuous validation and expert review are recommended before clinical deployment.
